**Figure 2. Experimental coverage and the collapse diagnostic.**
(A) Distinct Track-A seeds per dataset × encoding × regime; the digit is the exact
count. An empty cell is model collapse under that regime, not an experiment that
was skipped. (B) Every completed final-round draw, plotted as predictive margin
over the majority baseline against attribution norm ‖φ‖₂; the dashed line is the
Track-A threshold (margin > 0.02) and the shaded band is the Track-B region.
On Wisconsin collapse and near-zero attribution norm coincide, which is the
failure mode the Track-A gate exists to catch. **On Pima they do not**: collapsed
runs there have norms overlapping the functional ones, so the accuracy-based gate
does work the norm alone would not.

**Figure 3. RQ1 attribution fidelity under federation.**
(A) Final-round JSD between federated and centralized global attributions, by
encoding and regime. Hatched "n/e" cells have fewer than three Track-A draws and
are not estimated. The red mark on the colour bar is the ≈0.17 estimator-noise
floor of Section 5.5; most Wisconsin cell-to-cell differences sit at or below it.
(B) Per-draw accuracy difference against attribution distance, all Track-A draws
of both datasets. The shaded band is below the estimator floor. Spearman ρ = −0.03
(p = 0.73, n = 158): explanation divergence is **not** a by-product of the
federated-versus-centralized accuracy gap, which is what RQ1's "beyond the
accuracy cost of federation" requires.

**Figure 4. RQ2 round-to-round attribution stability.** Unchanged from the
validated manuscript figure; renamed only.

**Figure 5. RQ3 local-to-global explanation divergence.**
(A) D_LG by regime for both datasets; digits are Track-A seed counts, pale hatched
bars have fewer than five, open triangles mark cells with fewer than three (not
estimated, not plotted as zero). (B) The same quantity resolved per client and
round for one representative run. Under IID the four clients sit in a narrow band;
under α = 0.5 the divergence is carried by a single client (Client 2) in the early
rounds, which the aggregate mean in panel A cannot show.

**Figure 6. RQ4 membership leakage through released explanations.**
(A) Direct-attack AUC, mean ± 1 SD across seeds; the hatched bar is the Pima
cross-dataset check. (B) The per-seed values behind those means, with the
shadow-model control. Leakage is systematic rather than driven by a few seeds,
and the shadow attack sits at chance for every encoding — a shadow model trained
without federation does not transfer to the federated target.
*Note:* per-sample attack scores were not logged, so ROC curves cannot be drawn
from the committed results; this panel is the strongest statement the existing
logs support.

**Figure 7. RQ4 privacy–utility frontier of explanation release.**
Direct-attack AUC against retained explanation utility, one line per encoding,
four noise budgets. Error bars are ±1 SD across the sweep's five seeds. Angle sits
on a distinctly lower frontier: at equal utility it leaks less. No budget puts all
four encodings at chance while leaving usable explanation.
*Derived quantity:* the utility axis is a Monte-Carlo estimate of cos(φ, φ+noise)
under the sweep's own mechanism and per-encoding scale (Δf_emp = 0.229 / 0.262 /
0.344 / 0.287), evaluated on the logged clean Track-A vectors of the same five
seeds, 4000 draws, RNG seed 20260921. Values in `fig07_utility_derived.csv`.
The budget parameter indexes a comparable noise level; it is **not** a formal
(ε, δ) guarantee.

**Figure 8. Q-SHAP attribution behaviour.**
(A) Share of |φ| mass per principal component across rounds, averaged over Track-A
IID seeds. Every encoding concentrates on PC1 within about two rounds and holds —
this is the dominant-component lock-in that Figure 4's rank correlation measures
indirectly. (B) Mean |φ| per component, federated against centralized, on one
shared scale.
