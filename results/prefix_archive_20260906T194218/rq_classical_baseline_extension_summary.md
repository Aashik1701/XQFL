# Classical FedAvg Baseline — Extension Results (15 seeds, Wisconsin + Pima)

## Protocol (identical to the quantum pipeline)
- Classifier: matched-parameter classical MLP (13 params; same count as quantum VQC + linear head)
- Shared server-fit PCA(4) + MinMax([0, π]) preprocessing (fit once, never refit)
- IID partitioning, K = 4 clients
- FedAvg, 15 rounds × 3 local epochs; centralized reference 45 epochs on pooled data
- Attribution: `explain.q_shap.q_shap_values` (N_GLOBAL_BG=20, N_PERM=8, averaged over
  N_GLOBAL_EXPLAIN=10 test samples)
- Metric: `explain.metrics.js_divergence` (JSD, abs + epsilon=1e-10, base-2)
- Seeds: full 15-seed protocol [1,7,42,123,999] + [11,13,17,19,23,29,31,37,43,47]

## Results

### Wisconsin (n = 15)
- Classical JSD(fed, cent): **0.1858 ± 0.1453**  (SE = 0.0375)
- Classical fed accuracy: 0.9146 ± 0.1163; cent 0.8906 ± 0.1352
- Test majority baseline: 0.6316

**Directional, not resolved:** the matched-parameter classical MLP's federated-vs-centralized
attribution JSD is numerically higher than all four quantum encodings on Wisconsin, but the
gap does not reach two standard errors at n = 15 (max ratio 1.26). The result is directional
evidence that the quantum fidelity figures are not merely "any model at this capacity" — but
it is not conclusive on its own.

### Pima (n = 15)
- Classical JSD(fed, cent): **0.1608 ± 0.1467** (heavily contaminated by collapse)
- Classical fed accuracy: 0.6991 ± 0.0419; cent 0.6961 ± 0.0488
- Test majority baseline: 0.6494
- Classical fed accuracy beats majority by only **+4.98 pp**
- 5 of 15 seeds (1, 7, 31, 43, 47) collapse to exactly the 64.94% majority at BOTH fed and
  central levels (JSD = 0).

**Capacity finding (confirms phase9a Check 1):** the matched-13-param classical model barely
beats the majority baseline on Pima and collapses outright in 5/15 seeds — the same
capacity-limited behavior as the quantum angle/IQP encodings on Pima. This supports the
framing that the Pima weakness is the parameter/feature budget (4 PCA + 13 params), not the
quantum encoding specifically.

## Interpretation for the DA-1 circularity concern

1. On Wisconsin, the classical JSD (0.186) sits at or above the quantum encodings (0.13–0.16);
   this is directional but not conclusive evidence that the low quantum distortion is
   quantum-specific rather than a generic property of FedAvg at this capacity.
2. On Pima, the classical model reproduces the quantum collapse (5/15 seeds to majority),
   consistent with the thesis that low capacity — shared by classical and quantum alike — is
   the binding constraint on the harder dataset.
3. Net: the classical control **partially** rebuts the circularity concern (metric is mildly
   sensitive to architecture; quantum models sit at the low-fidelity-distortion end) but does
   not fully settle it. The paper should report it honestly as directional evidence.
